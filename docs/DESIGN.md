## Glass recipe
One pane, used by tile, menu, banner and sheet:

```css
border: 1px solid var(--portal-border);
border-radius: var(--portal-radius);
background: var(--portal-pane-sheen), var(--portal-pane-fill);
box-shadow: var(--portal-pane-shadow);
```

The blur lives on the source (the gradient canvas), not on the panes. A single-instance surface such as the Sheet may carry its own `backdrop-filter`, kept under 20px.

## Type
Weight does hierarchy, not colour. Three text tiers, six sizes. Every count sets `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums`. Inputs are 16px so iOS Safari does not zoom on focus.

## Contrast
Measured with the workspace's contrast.mjs: the shipped pncl preset's brightest stop, then the pane fill, then the sheen, then the text. At fill 0.68 the 0.80 tier holds 4.76:1 and the 0.60 tier 3.45:1. Blur never counts as separation; text always sits on a fill. Those are the worst case, the gradient dashboard; any other surface is re-measured against that surface (R27). Every portal page now carries that same gradient (Shell, Backdrop), so those are the pane's figures on every page. The flat `--ink` reading, 13.42:1 at 0.95, 9.94:1 at 0.80 and 6.27:1 at 0.60, is what the pane falls back to on a browser without WebGL2, where the canvas paints nothing and the page's own base shows. Text that sits on no fill is the case to watch: sampled off a 1440x900 frame the backdrop renders no brighter than `rgb(89, 75, 61)`, which holds white 0.62 at 4.40:1 and white 0.80 at 6.09:1, but the preset's brightest stop is `rgb(232, 205, 176)` and a frame that drifts there would take both under 1.5:1. That is why the masthead and the bars take `--portal-bar-fill`, and why the seven runs that had no fill once the backdrop went in now take it too: the sub-page header itself above 620px (below that it is already a sticky bar with the same fill), the page lede under it, the profile details lede, the profile footer, and the two legacy headers on brand assets and the comp agreement. On `--portal-bar-fill` they read white 0.62 = 7.43:1, `--steel` = 9.78:1 and `--bone` = 15.30:1, and 6.57:1, 8.00:1 and 12.52:1 under the sheen's maximum. No text run on a portal page sits on the bare backdrop now; anything added later has to bring its own fill.

## Motion
300 to 420ms on `--portal-ease-out`. Tilt is off on coarse pointers and under `prefers-reduced-motion`; gradient drift and reveal wipes stop under `prefers-reduced-motion` (coarse pointers run the canvas at 20fps, 0.5 dpr). `--portal-duration` drops to 0ms under `prefers-reduced-motion`, so anything on the token needs no second rule.

## Focus
`:focus-visible` only: `outline: var(--portal-focus-ring); outline-offset: var(--portal-focus-offset)`. Never remove an outline without replacing it.

## Forms with a PDF canvas
The ICA and the W-9 are pdf.js canvases with AcroForm inputs overlaid on them. `src/styles/portal-forms.css` styles the chrome around that canvas and nothing inside it.

| Part | Class | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Document frame | `.pforms-doc` | The pane recipe. `overflow: clip`, never `hidden`: hidden computes `overflow-y` to auto and makes the frame a scrollport that never scrolls, which stops the pager inside it from ever sticking. |
| Section jumps | `.pforms-jumps` | Segmented in tab mode over the document (Introduction / Signature / Debit-Check, Form / Instructions). The jump items carry `controls`, and the canvas host is their panel on a page a jump owns: 3 of the ICA's 14, 2 of the W-9's 6. On every other page no tab is selected, so `Segmented`'s `panelAria` gives the host `role="group"` and `aria-label="Page n of m"` rather than pointing `aria-labelledby` at a tab that renders `aria-selected="false"` (WCAG 4.1.2). |
| Pager | `.pforms-pager` | Prev, a 16px page-number input, "of n", then the action slot. Sticky at every width, and above the bottom nav at 620px and below: at page-width scale a letter page runs about 1290px tall on a desktop pane, so a pager pinned to the bottom of the frame would sit below the fold. In a tile reveal `portal-tile.css` drives `.portal-row` from the same rules as the `.ptile-link` it replaced, so the 44px floor, the full width and the 8px radius apply on a page but not in a card menu. `.ptile-reveal-body .portal-pane` strips the pane's padding, border, radius, fill and shadow for the same reason: the card is already the glass surface, and PortalReferralPanel renders one Pane for both the dashboard reveal and the profile's Team tab, where the pane IS the surface.

The profile's Licensing and Documents tabs add one page-level pattern rather than a primitive: `.portal-dropzone` in `portal-profile.css`, a `<label>` whose `<input type="file">` covers it at zero opacity. A file input is already a drop target, so the drag, the tap and the native picker all work without a drag handler, the label's copy names the control, and the ring goes on the zone through `:focus-within`. The chosen file reads back as a Chip under the zone, never as colour alone.

The Carrier logins tab adds the second page-level pattern: two surfaces for one list, swapped at 620px by `display` alone. Below that width each carrier is a ListRow carrying the name and the writing number, and a tap opens the Sheet. Above it the table returns with 44px rows, its cell padding horizontal only so a 44px control fills a row rather than adding to it. One surface is displayed at a time, so assistive tech meets a single list. The Sheet is the editor at both widths, since the primitive is already a bottom sheet on a phone and a 420px right panel on a desktop. The password is masked in both places, and a reveal button sits beside it. The table cell masks it with a fixed eight dots, so the cell never reports the real length, and hides them from assistive tech because the reveal button's own label carries the state; the Sheet uses a native password input, which masks per character the way every password field does. The three fields take `autocomplete="off"`, because a carrier login is not the agent's own login and a browser password manager should not capture it.

## State map
`/portal/state-map` answers two questions between calls: is PNCL active in the client's state, and is the agent licensed there. The page is a thin container (`src/pages/PortalStateMap.tsx`: every hook and derivation) around a presentational `StateMapView` that takes plain props, so the harness renders every state from fixtures. The selection and the filter live in the URL as `?state=TX` and `?filter=Pending`, written with `replace`; a `?state=` on load beats the address-state default and opens its card, which is also the deep link other pages use.

| Width | Layout |
|---|---|
| 620px and below | The page does not scroll. Header, the 44px sub-page bar (its `aside` holds the 44px info button that opens the legend), the map card filling the rest, and a SnapSheet docked above the tab bar: peek (search and filters), detail (the answer card, content height capped at 50dvh), full (search and filters pinned, the list scrolling inside). The preselected address state is only outlined; the sheet never opens on load. |
| 621 to 960px | Map card on top, the finder below it in normal flow, the list in two columns. |
| 961px and up | A sticky 360px panel as tall as the viewport, scrolling inside (search and filters fixed at its top), first in the DOM; the map card takes the rest, capped at the viewport height. |

One finder component renders either in the sheet or in the panel, never both.

### The map
`src/components/portal/StateMapSvg.tsx`, lazy. Inline SVG from `us-atlas/states-albers-10m.json`, which is pre-projected with y pointing down, so topojson-client's `feature()` output becomes path strings with no projection code. The 9,348 points are parsed once at module scope in the lazy chunk. It replaced the Three.js canvas: the camera was orthographic and straight down, so WebGL bought nothing, and SVG draws crisp at native dpr, hit-tests through the DOM and carries real labels. `StateAvailabilityCanvas.tsx` stays in the tree, unused, so a rollback is an import swap.

Fills keep their own three colours rather than the swatches in `STATE_AVAILABILITY_META`, which sit at 1.48:1 (Active to Pending) and 2.31:1 (Active to Inactive) and merge for a viewer with red-green colour blindness. Active moved a step darker than the canvas's `#27865a` so the 11px white code on it clears 4.5:1.

| Encoding | Value | Measured |
|---|---|---|
| Active fill | `#258258` | 3.16:1 against Inactive; white label 4.76:1 (was 4.52:1 on `#27865a`) |
| Pending fill | `#fbdf9d` under a 45deg hatch in `#7a5c14`, an SVG pattern whose pitch resets on zoom end to 6px on screen | 3.66:1 against Active; the hatch 4.79:1 on its fill; dark label `#171a20` 13.40:1 |
| Inactive fill | `#212730` | 11.55:1 against Pending; white label 15.03:1 |
| Unavailable | `#3d4654` on every state when live data failed | white label 9.53:1 |
| Edges | Two meshes from topojson's `mesh()`: `#171a20` at 0.85 on any border touching a bright fill, `#f4f0df` at 0.55 between two dark fills and on a dark state's coastline | 1px on screen at every zoom |
| Licensed | a constant 16px ring-check badge: near-black `#101318` disc, cream `#f4f0df` ring and check | the halo carries it on every fill |
| Selected | 2.5px white 0.95 outline, drawn last | no dimming of the rest |

Every stroke is `vector-effect: non-scaling-stroke`; labels, badges, edges and outlines take `pointer-events: none`, so a tap lands on a state fill. Codes are Manrope 11px/700 on screen at every zoom (the SVG font size is set from the current scale) with a `paint-order: stroke` halo in the state's own fill, so the hatch never cuts a glyph. A code shows only where the state's shorter on-screen side is at least 40px and the pole of inaccessibility (the interior point farthest from the edge, precomputed per state in `state-map-geometry.ts`) leaves room for it, which keeps every label inside its own state; that pole also places FL, LA, MI, KY, VA and ID on their broad bodies without hand offsets. The badge shows once the state is 16px on screen and pairs with the code, side by side or stacked in a tall state. Labels and badges hide while the camera moves and return sized for where it stopped.

Camera: the viewBox. Zoom 1 fits the atlas's own bbox (x -58 to 958, y 13 to 607, so the Aleutians are not clipped) plus 6 units of margin at the stage's aspect. `clampView` keeps it inside that frame at zoom 1 to 8, the old `cameraCenter` margin maths, with extra room at the bottom for the part a sheet covers. Pinch zooms around its midpoint, one finger pans once zoomed, ctrl or cmd plus wheel zooms at the cursor (a trackpad pinch arrives that way) and a plain wheel scrolls the page; `touch-action: none` sits on the stage only. There is no double-tap zoom, which would hold every tap 300ms. Fly-to is a rAF tween of the four viewBox numbers over 380ms on `--portal-ease-out`, fitting the state with 24px of air into the part of the stage the sheet leaves visible, zoom 1 to 6; reduced motion jumps.

Taps: a drag past 8px is never a pick. A tap samples the state under the finger and two rings around it (10 and 20px, eight points each) through `elementsFromPoint`. One state in reach selects it; open map clears the selection; a crowd zooms 2.5x into the tap below zoom 4 and picks under the finger (else the nearest) from zoom 4. A finger squarely on a state already 44px on its shorter side selects it at any zoom, which keeps every state outside the rail within two taps of load.

Controls: zoom in and out fused into one pill with a 1px divider, reset as its own square, 6px apart, all 44px, horizontal in a 56px band across the top of the phone card (so nothing overlaps the map at zoom 1) and a vertical stack bottom right from 621px. At a limit a button is disabled at 0.5 opacity. The map is one `role="img"` stop named with the counts; the list is how a keyboard or a screen reader picks a state.

Small-state rail (`SmallStateRail.tsx`): VT, NH, MA, RI, CT, NJ, DE, MD and DC, the nine with a shorter side under 50 atlas units, as 44px buttons under the map. Each carries its code, a 10px swatch of its fill (hatched for Pending) and the badge, and a name like "Rhode Island, Inactive, Licensed". It scrolls sideways when it runs out of room.

Hover (fine pointers): a 160px `role="status"` readout, name, status Chip and licensed mark, parked beside the state's anchor, clamped inside the card and flipped above near the bottom.

### The finder and the card
Search is a `type="search"` Field (label kept for assistive tech, hidden visually), 16px, 44px tall, a leading icon in a non-interactive slot and a 44px clear that refocuses the input; WebKit's own cancel button is hidden. Focus sends the phone sheet to full, Enter picks the first match, Escape clears and blurs, `/` focuses it when no field has focus. Filters are one `Segmented` in radiogroup mode with the sliding pill: All, Active, Pending, Inactive and Licensed, each with the Chip's glyph and a count (no counts while loading; only All and Licensed when live data failed). Items are 32px on a 44px track, and an `::after` grows each hit area to 44px. `matchesFilter` in `state-map-filter.ts` is the one predicate for the list and the map; a state the filter leaves out fades to 0.2 on the map over 200ms. The result count rolls each changed digit and reads as the final number. Rows are 44px ListRows: code, name, status Chip plus a Licensed Chip, and a 3px bar in the status fill as a second cue; `aria-current` marks the selection.

The answer card (`StateDetail.tsx`): code badge, the name at 22/600, then label and value rows: PNCL status (Chip and the status sentence), Your license (ring-check, "Licensed on your profile" and the number, or an empty ring, "No license recorded on your profile." and an Add a license row to the licensing tab) and Updated (hidden when empty). It states the two facts and stops: carrier appointments are not in this data. A keyboard pick moves focus to the card heading and the card's close brings it back to the row. Moving between states fades the content out in 160ms and in over 240ms with a 6px rise inside a wrapper held at its height; reduced motion swaps instantly. In the desktop panel the card sits on a recessed `rgba(0, 0, 0, 0.28)` fill, which holds the faint Updated date at 4.66:1; in the sheet it sits on `--portal-bar-fill`.

States kept: loading (the outline at white 0.06, three row skeletons, a visually hidden "Loading state availability…", the rail's height reserved), live data failed (a compact notice at the top of the map card on a phone and of the panel above it, "Try again" wired to `reload`, a neutral map, rows reading "Unavailable"), no match, the Licensed filter with no licenses (an EmptyState with Add licenses), and the legend sheet (the old page lede, one row per status with its swatch, and the badge).

### Direct deposit

The third form carries no canvas. `.pforms-dd-col` holds the loading pane, the submitted pane, the form and the "About this form" aside in one 560px column, which stops a five-character ZIP field short of the 1320px wrap. Inside it the controls are a single stack of Field, one per row at every width, with `inputmode="numeric"` and no `autocomplete` on the account and routing numbers. The checking and savings pair and the authorization line take `.pforms-ack` rows, the same 44px target the signing sheet uses, inside a `<fieldset>` whose legend names the group.

`.pforms-submit-bar` shares the pager's sticky recipe, its `overflow-x: clip` on the page wrapper and its lift above the bottom nav at 620px and below. `validatePortalDirectDepositForm` returns one message for the whole form and `src/lib` is read-only, so the page maps that message back to a control id by prefix and moves focus there. Mount `<PortalBackground />` as the first child of the page wrapper on every portal page. The dashboard, the calendar and the state map (through StateMapView) render through `PortalBentoMain` instead, the dashboard's own frame with its tuned canvas and vignette, and PortalAuthLayout carries its own canvas; none of them may gain a second. The canvas pauses itself offscreen, on a hidden tab and under `prefers-reduced-motion`, where it draws one frame and holds, and `fallbackColor="transparent"` leaves the page's own dark base showing when WebGL2 is unavailable.

Mounting it is not enough, and this trap was hit once already, in prototyping. A portal page paints an opaque fill over the fixed layer twice over, from `.home2-page:has(.portal-dash)` on the wrapper and `.home2-page .portal-dash` on `<main>`, both in `home2.css`, so the canvas renders into a layer nobody can see. `portal-shell.css` lets it through: `.home2-page:has(> .portal-backdrop)` keeps the dark base, and `.home2-page:has(> .portal-backdrop) > main` goes transparent and takes `position: relative; z-index: 1`. The position is not decoration; `z-index` applies to positioned boxes only, and the selector needs its trailing element to clear `.home2-page .portal-dash` at 0-2-0. Only `<main>` is raised: `.grain` and `.portal-bottom-nav` are already fixed with their own `z-index`, and `position: relative` would break both. Verify a page by screenshot, never by reading the CSS. Twelve pages carry it; `src/components/portal/PortalBackground.test.tsx` scans every `Portal*.tsx` with a `home2-page` wrapper and fails if one lacks the mount or has it anywhere but first, so a new page cannot ship flat, and checks the three frame pages the other way round.

Letting the gradient through also moves every fill-less text run off the flat `--ink` and onto a live canvas, which is the second half of the same trap. See Contrast: the five runs that had no fill now take `--portal-bar-fill` from the same block in `portal-shell.css`. A new run that sits directly on the page wrapper needs one too.

`--portal-bar-fill` (`rgb(28, 22, 17)`, the pane fill colour made solid) backs all three shell surfaces. A fixed or sticky bar sits over scrolling content, so the translucent pane fill would let whatever scrolled under it set the contrast. On the solid fill white 0.95 reads 16.20:1 and the tab bar's white 0.52 label 5.58:1; under the sheen's maximum they are 13.36:1 and 5.08:1, so the quiet tab clears 4.5:1 across the whole bar. The 0.52 is declared on `.portal-bottom-nav`, the container, not on the item, for the reason in the last bullet below. Declared on the item it never reached the glyphs, so the figure described a colour nobody could see.

The masthead takes the same fill. It scrolls rather than sticks, but it sits over the gradient's brightest region: the wall light is anchored at 16% 4%, top left, under the logo and the title. Measured on the bare backdrop (pncl brightest stop `rgb(232, 205, 176)`) white reads 1.49:1 at 0.95, 1.41:1 at 0.80 and 1.30:1 at 0.60, all three below every threshold. On `--portal-bar-fill` under the sheen the same tiers read 13.36:1, 9.90:1 and 6.25:1. Padding is 12px 16px, matching the `.pbanner` that sits directly under it.

## Mobile rules
| Rule | Number |
|---|---|
| Tap targets | 44x44 CSS px minimum, 8px between adjacent targets |
| Inputs | font-size 16px, correct `autocomplete` and `inputmode` |
| Bottom nav | 3 to 5 tabs; `padding-bottom: env(safe-area-inset-bottom)` on it and on every sticky bar |
| Text on glass | 4.5:1 body, 3:1 large and UI text; back text with a fill |
| backdrop-filter | under 20px, never on multi-instance surfaces |
| Map | pinch needs +/- buttons; hover info reachable by tap; the map is one labelled image and the list is the keyboard path; small states get a 44px rail |
| Forms | single column, label above field, 2 to 4 inputs per wizard screen, error next to its field, focus the first error on submit |
| Progress | 3 to 7 visible steps; show the 5 stages, not all 31 steps |
| Skeletons | mirror the final layout, only for loads under 10s |
| Motion | `prefers-reduced-motion` kills tilt, gradient drift and reveal wipes |

## Anti-patterns
- Hover-only information. Anything a hover shows must be reachable by tap and keyboard.
- Accent as a field. `--portal-accent` is a spark for urgent states, never a background.
- backdrop-filter on multi-instance surfaces. One source blur beats N receiver blurs.
- Blur as separation. It never counts toward contrast; put a fill behind text.
- Colour as hierarchy. Change the weight or the size before the alpha.
- Dropping the focus ring.
